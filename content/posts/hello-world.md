Hi! I'm Ollie. I'm a Harvard student (technically on leave for a semester at the time of writing but whatever), and I like doing CS and Math, reading Sci-Fi, coding various things (I still code by hand sometimes even in the year of our lord twenty twenty six), singing, running, trying new things and listening to my friends talk about their interests. Oh - and parentheticals, I really love parentheticals (as you'll no doubt notice if you read my writing for any amount of time). I think a lot about AI and AI safety, as well as associated questions like "if we could choose any way for the world to be set up - what should we choose?" and "how much time/effort should you spend trying to figure out what to do vs actually working towards your best guess", etc. 

A few months ago, I found myself sitting in the middle seat of a uhaul, traveling through the California hills away from San Francisco, towards Boston. The reason for this has to do with various life circumstances that I'll get into later, but it was at this time I came to the conclusion that my life, and the world, had gotten quite crazy and I really wished I could be writing down more of my thoughts and experiences so that I could look back on them, and so that other people could perhaps have a way of understanding me more (and hopefully learn a thing or two?). So, I've decided to start this blog. 

I think I have a lot of reasons for starting a blog, including (but not limited to)
1. I enjoy writing for fun, especially when I just get to write about whatever I've been thinking about recently. 
2. Writing is an important way to signal something about my character to people who might want to work with me. I'm a little averse to signalling in this way (maybe just because most decent sources of signal get goodharts lawed very quickly and then feel meaningless), but I've come to have a grudging respect for the ways in which having public displays of character can be very useful when it comes to helping society organize itself more effectively. I.e. if there's someone I want to do research with, they can just read my blog, get a sense of who I am and what I stand for, and then make a much more educated guess as to whether we would work well together.  Writing style isn't really something that can be faked (I mean you can use LLMs but that's kinda counterproductive) so it still provides meaningful signal, even if my anti-posturing heuristics don't like it.
3. I'll have an easily accesible repository of my past thouughts which will allow me to better remember my past thinking and analyze how its changed over time. (Technically I wouldn't need to write publicly for this one, but see #4). 
4. Writing *for* something gives me motivation to write in the first place. I could just write to myself (and have done so to a decent extent) but it's better practice to write for some sort of audience, and it motivates me to actually flesh things out and put effort into it. 
5. I can potentially get feedback from other people on my ideas and my writing, and thereby improve my writing and thinking (if someone actually reads my blog lol). 
6. Writing lets me spend time organizing my thinking in a way that I potentially otherwise wouldn't. Producing decent writing is a pretty good proxy task for thinking clearly about the world. 
7. Writing is a skill that seems unlikely to be fully automated by AI (at least not before something like Brain-Computer-Interfaces become a thing), as it is mostly about getting what is in your head into someone else's head. Unless AI systems can go into your head and get it out more effectively (somehow) writing seems to be safe from automation. (Insofar as it is about communicating things which are unique to your perspective). Some, more instrumental forms of writing, will be and already have been automated, but all writing? Probably not. Also, even as AI systems get better and better at writing, writing well still gives significant relative advantage when trying to communicate. 

I'm probably not going to focus too much on making my writing super accessible or polished, largely because I'm writing for the sake of writing and want to be able to use my most natural voice and not overthink things. Unfortunately this does mean that some things are probably going to end up written in techspeak vernacular (like I used the word heuristic up there - you'll probably also hear me saying things like "non trivial" "orthogonal" "world model" and various other words that some of my friends make fun of me for using). Maybe I'll even start saying "intuition pump" because I've been listening to too much Dwarkesh :D. 



Anyway, I think I'll close this one out by saying what I hope to write about in the future, (to hopefully motivate me further to actually do so). 

1. Agency - I've heard people throw the term agency around a lot (and abuse it in various different ways), so I've been thinking about what agency even means and how/when it is (and isn't) a relevant concept. 
2. Continual learning - I think this is probably the biggest piece left unfound in the AGI puzzle - it's probably the key to achieving human level sample efficiency, and I've been thinking a lot about how such a system could work, partially by attempting to reverse engineer something of how my own brain works. 
3. Backstory - the promised backstory post (possibly a series on different topics) going through my thoughts on growing up homeschooled (in small town ohio), going to harvard, founding a startup, etc. 
4. Thoughts on various research papers as I read them - hopefully I can leverage my blog into also reading more research papers, thinking about them, possibly trying to replicate them, and generally getting a deeper understanding of the field. 

We'll see! I hope to spend the next couple months writing a lot and fleshing out my ideas, and possibly that will turn out to be useful. 

That's All Folks!! See ya in the next one

Ollie Out
