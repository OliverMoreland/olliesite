Whenever I'm trying to make sense of something really complicated and hard to figure out, I often organize my thinking by starting with things that I strongly believe to be true. (as in, would be willing to bet my life that they are true). This kind of combines the idea that you should "start with certainty" and also that you should break hard thinking down into smaller steps. I use this technique when I'm thinking about life decisions and when I'm thinking about the world and tryng to discern truth.  Say, for example, I'm trying to think about political issues. If I'm trying to think about immigration, I'll start by laying about basic statements, like "it's bad to cause people suffering," "forcing people to leave their homes typically causes people suffering," "people are more likely to enter a country if they think they will have a good life there," "if you take a country, and add more workers to it, the amount of work getting done will probably go up," or "cultural differences can cause conflicts." A good analysis of the problem will take into account all of these statements and more. Even though I might like some of these statements more than others (maybe I believe that the cultural conflicts caused by immigration aren't really that bad), I don't actually disagree with the truth values of any of them. This helps me bound and sometimes parameterize the space of possible conclusions.
